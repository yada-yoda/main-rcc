Talent Agency Submission Prep

Working list of talent agencies across Chicago, NYC, LA, and Georgia — what to submit, when to submit, and a tracking template. Maintained outside the public site so it stays personal.

Last reviewed: 2026-05-23 · verify every address & policy on the agency’s own site before mailing
Read this first. Agency addresses, submission policies, and even agency names change. This page is a research starting point, not a directory. Always verify the current submission address & policy on each agency’s own website before sending materials. A submission to a closed office reads as “does not do basic homework” and gets you blacklisted.
Your edge in Chicago. Second City Conservatory training matters here. Improv-friendly agencies (Big Mouth, Stewart, Paonessa) actively scout the Second City pipeline. Mention your conservatory completion + grad revues prominently in your cover letter.
Multi-market reality check. Submitting to agencies in markets where you don’t live is harder than submitting locally. Without “Local Hire” status (you cover your own travel/lodging) in NYC, LA, or Atlanta, most agents won’t consider taking you on because they can’t pitch you for last-minute auditions. Realistic multi-market plays:
  • Chicago primary, NYC/LA secondary — build credits locally; submit to other markets when you have something they can’t source locally.
  • Self-tape-only reps in remote markets — some boutique agencies will sign you for self-tape auditions only, no in-person meetings required.
  • Local hire in additional markets — add “Local hire in NYC” to your resume only if you have a verifiable address / contact there.
  • Relocate first, then submit — most actors who break in NYC/LA had moved there first. Submitting from Chicago to an LA agency is a real long shot.

01 Submission Materials — have all of these ready before submitting to anyone

Required for every submission

  • Headshot. 8×10 color print for mail submissions; 800px+ JPG for email/portal. Multiple looks (commercial smile + dramatic) is a plus.
  • Resume. Single-page PDF for digital. Printed copy stapled to back of headshot for mail submissions (alignment matters — trim to 8×10 if printed on larger paper).
  • Reel. Vimeo or YouTube link. Under 3 minutes ideal. Best moment first 15 seconds.
  • Cover letter / email body. Under 150 words. Who you are, why this agency specifically, what you bring, polite ask for representation meeting.

Often required

  • SAG-AFTRA status. “Non-Union” is fine to state. Don’t pretend.
  • Vocal range / dance level / special skill demo (when relevant to the role types you target).
  • Photo gallery link (Actors Access, Casting Networks profile).

03 How to actually submit

By email (most common today)

  • Subject line: Submission — Frank Rizzo / [Type] — The Second City Conservatory
  • Body: 3 short paragraphs.
    1. One line of who you are + why this agency specifically.
    2. One short paragraph of credits + training summary (mention the most recent reel project).
    3. One line linking your headshot, resume, and reel + thank you for their time.
  • Attachments: headshot JPG + resume PDF. Don’t paste reel as attachment; link it.

By mail (still required by some)

  • Manila envelope (do not fold the headshot)
  • Inside: printed 8×10 color headshot with resume stapled to the back (top & bottom), short cover letter on top, business card (optional)
  • Address to the specific agent if you know one; otherwise “Submissions” or “Talent Department”
  • USPS first class is fine. Don’t use Priority/Express; reads as desperate.

Via online portal

  • Some agencies use Casting Networks or proprietary portals. Read their site’s submission page exactly.
  • Upload current materials; don’t leave old reels lying around your profile.
Cardinal rules: One submission per agency (not multiple agents within the same agency). Don’t follow up before 4 weeks. Don’t pretend to be referred when you weren’t.

04 Phased submission plan

The goal is steady progress, not blast-out. Submissions evaluated against your current materials. If you’re unhappy with your reel or headshot, fix that BEFORE you submit (you only get one chance with each agency).

  1. Phase 0 — Materials audit. Headshot recent? Reel reflects current ability? Resume PDF accurate (we just shipped this)? If any of these are weak, fix first.
  2. Phase 1 — Tier 1. Submit to all 4 top-tier agencies in the same week. Email if accepted; mail if their site says mail-only. Track everything in the table below.
  3. Phase 2 — Wait. 4 weeks minimum. Don’t panic. Keep auditioning, taking classes, building credits.
  4. Phase 3 — Follow-up Tier 1. One brief email per agency that hasn’t responded. Mention any new credit or class since first submission as the “reason to follow up.” That’s why ongoing classes matter.
  5. Phase 4 — Tier 2 submissions. If no Tier 1 bites by week 8, submit to Tier 2 agencies. Same process.
  6. Phase 5 — Tier 3 + boutique. Week 12 onwards if needed.
  7. Phase 6 — Ongoing engagement. Even with rejections, keep agencies on your radar. Update them when you book significant work. Once a year is reasonable.

05 Concrete timeline

Week 0

Materials audit

Headshot current? Reel ≤3 min and shows your range? Resume accurate (PDF + Actors Access profile match)? Cover letter draft written?

Week 1

Research week

Visit each Tier 1 agency’s website. Note current submission policy, exact address/email, who’s reading submissions. Update the tracker below with what you find.

Week 2

Tier 1 batch

Send all four Tier 1 submissions in the same week. Personalize the “why this agency” line per submission. Log dates in tracker.

Weeks 3–6

Wait + build

No checking in. Audition, take classes, build credits. Update your IMDb / Actors Access if new credits land.

Week 7

Tier 1 follow-up

Single short email to any Tier 1 agency that hasn’t replied. Lead with a recent credit or class as the reason for the touch.

Week 8

Tier 2 batch

Submit to Tier 2 agencies. Same format. Note: it’s fine to be in conversation with multiple tiers at once if Tier 1 hasn’t closed.

Week 12+

Tier 3 + reassess

If no representation yet, submit to Tier 3. Also reassess: are materials the issue, or is timing? Sometimes a quick reel re-cut beats endless re-submissions.

Quarterly

Maintenance

Once represented or not, update IMDb / Actors Access / rizzo.cc with new credits each quarter. Reach out to your top non-responders annually with material updates.
